# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity

## A Comparative Framework for Extracting Universal Ethics from the World's Enduring Religions

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey  
**Date:** 2026  
**Paper:** I of IV

---

## 1. Introduction

Human civilizations have risen, flourished, and collapsed throughout recorded history. Among the many variables that contribute to societal stability—geography, resource abundance, technological innovation, and military power—one factor remains conspicuously under-examined in quantitative longitudinal study: **the moral code embedded in a civilization's dominant religious tradition**.

This paper proposes a new comparative project that re-examines the world's eight largest and most historically enduring religions, not as theological systems, but as repositories of practical ethical instruction.

---

## 2. Central Hypothesis

The central hypothesis is straightforward:

> **Religions that have sustained large populations over millennia contain core ethical principles that directly enhance social stability, reduce internal conflict, and protect property and personhood.**

These principles, when internalized as cultural norms, function as informal legal codes that:

- Lower transaction costs
- Increase trust
- Discourage behaviours that fracture communities

The longevity of both the religion and its associated civilizations may therefore be partially explained by the **utilitarian value of these ethical lessons**, rather than solely by doctrinal appeal or divine sanction.

---

## 3. Methodological Approach

To test this hypothesis, we must strip religious ethics to their functional essence.

### Example: "Thou Shalt Not Steal"

| Layer | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|
| Theological | Not examined as divine decree |
| Narrative | Not examined as part of covenantal story |
| **Functional** | **"Do not take what belongs to another without consent."** |

### Secondary Effects of This Single Precept

When widely observed, this generates predictable secondary effects:

- Respect for property rights
- Reduced retaliatory violence
- Lower judicial burdens
- Greater incentives for productive labour

These effects, in turn, correlate with:
- Economic growth
- Political tranquillity

The same analytical reduction will be applied to every ethical teaching extracted from each religion's primary sacred text.

---

## 4. Project Phases

The project proceeds in four phases, corresponding to subsequent papers in this series:

| Phase | Paper | Activity |
|-------|-------|----------|
| **I** | This paper | Selection and justification of the eight religions |
| **II** | Paper II | Systematic extraction of all prescriptive ethical statements from foundational scripture |
| **III** | Paper III | Translation and normalization into single, non-theological, plain-English statements of behaviour |
| **IV** | Paper IV | Cross-comparative mapping to identify universal, common, and unique ethics |

### Selection Criteria (Phase I)

Based on:
- Global adherent count
- Continuous historical practice (minimum 1,500 years)

### Ethical Extraction (Phase II)

Excluding:
- Ritual content
- Cosmological content
- Narrative content

### Normalization (Phase III)

Into:
- Single, non-theological statements
- Plain-English behavioural descriptions

### Cross-Comparison (Phase IV)

Identifying:
- **Universal ethics** (present in all eight)
- **Common ethics** (present in a majority)
- **Unique ethics** (present in a single tradition)

---

## 5. Clarification of Scope

### What This Paper Does NOT Argue

> Religion is the **only** or even the **primary** driver of civilizational success.

### What This Paper DOES Argue

> Enduring religions act as durable transmission vehicles for actionable social heuristics.

Where these heuristics align with known conditions for stable cooperation—reciprocity, non-aggression, honesty, care for kin and community—societies that adopted them may have gained a selective advantage over neighbouring groups with less coherent or less prosocial moral codes.

---

## 6. Ultimate Aim

The ultimate aim is **not** to champion any one faith, but to produce a **neutral, empirical catalogue of ethical commonalities across humanity's great religious traditions**.

### If Such Commonalities Exist and Correlate with Historical Stability

We may begin to speak not of "religious morality" but of **"civilizational ethics"** —a shared inheritance that:

- Transcends creed
- Offers practical lessons for contemporary social design

---

## 8. Conclusion

This paper proposes a comparative project that examines enduring religions as repositories of practical ethical instruction rather than as theological systems. The central hypothesis is that religions that have sustained large populations over millennia contain core ethical principles that directly enhance social stability.

The project will proceed through four phases: selection and justification, systematic extraction, translation and normalization, and cross-comparative mapping. The ultimate aim is a neutral, empirical catalogue of ethical commonalities across humanity's great religious traditions.

If such commonalities exist and correlate with historical stability, we may identify a shared inheritance of **civilizational ethics** that transcends creed and offers practical lessons for contemporary social design.
