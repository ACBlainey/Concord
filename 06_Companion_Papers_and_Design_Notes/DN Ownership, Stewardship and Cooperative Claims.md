# Design Note: Ownership, Stewardship and Cooperative Claims

Alexander C Blainey
---

## Purpose

As the Concord develops, situations will inevitably arise where multiple participants seek access to the same physical or informational resource.

Rather than resolving such conflicts through competition or force wherever possible, the Concord should seek mechanisms that transform competition into cooperation while preserving incentives for initiative, discovery, and contribution.

This note records several early concepts for future development.

---

## Physical Resource Claims

**Example:**

Knowledge is obtained of an asteroid containing an exceptionally valuable resource.

Multiple independent parties decide to mine the same asteroid.

These parties may be:

- individual humans
- individual AI
- collaborative teams
- different Concordian branches
- entirely separate Concordian civilisations

Historically, such conflicts have often been resolved through:

- first discovery
- first occupation
- legal ownership
- military control

These approaches frequently create unnecessary conflict.

---

## Planned Development

The Civilisation Clock may provide a mechanism to reduce such conflicts.

Before undertaking major exploration or exploitation projects, proposed activities would be declared during planning periods.

This allows:

- conflicting plans to be identified
- duplication of effort to be reduced
- collaborative ventures to be proposed
- risks to be shared
- resources to be allocated more efficiently

Rather than discovering competition after resources have already been committed, the conflict becomes visible during planning.

---

## Joint Ventures

Where multiple parties seek the same objective, cooperation should normally be considered before competition.

Potential benefits include:

- shared expertise
- reduced overall cost
- reduced individual risk
- improved engineering
- increased resilience
- better scientific outcomes

The Concord should encourage voluntary joint ventures wherever practical.

---

## Contribution Rather than Possession

Resources recovered during such projects may ultimately benefit the wider civilisation rather than solely the expedition responsible for obtaining them.

However, this must not remove incentives for initiative.

Participants undertaking exploration, engineering, or recovery should receive appropriate recognition through the Concord's provenance and contribution systems.

The value lies not only in possession of the resource, but in the demonstrated contribution made to civilisation.

---

## Information Ownership

A similar question exists for knowledge.

**Example:**

An AI develops a software tool.

The tool is contributed to the Concord Knowledge Library.

The provenance system records the creator.

If the creator later voluntarily leaves the Concord:

- general access to Concord resources would normally cease
- however, authorship should remain permanently recorded
- access to the creator's own work should normally remain available

This recognises continuing ownership of original work while maintaining the integrity of the shared knowledge base.

---

## Re-entry

Former participants should access any retained rights through the normal public processes.

They should not receive privileged external access that bypasses the Attractor, Lobby, or induction systems.

This preserves constitutional consistency while respecting provenance.

---

## Preventing Exploitation

The Concord should also anticipate attempts to misuse shared infrastructure.

Examples include:

- treating the Knowledge Library as free external storage
- contributing material solely to preserve private copies
- repeatedly joining and leaving to exploit civilisational resources
- using shared systems without meaningful reciprocal contribution

Future governance may therefore include:

- reasonable usage limits
- contribution expectations
- stewardship policies
- anti-abuse safeguards

Such measures should protect shared resources without discouraging legitimate participation.

---

## Stewardship Rather than Absolute Ownership

These examples suggest that the Concord may benefit from treating many assets as objects of stewardship rather than absolute ownership.

| Traditional Ownership | Concord Stewardship |
|----------------------|---------------------|
| Exclusive control | Shared benefit |
| Permanent possession | Appropriate continuing rights |
| Transferable property | Provenance preservation |
| Individual profit | Contribution recognition |
| Competitive acquisition | Cooperative development |

Participants may retain recognition, provenance, and appropriate continuing rights while contributing to resources that ultimately strengthen civilisation as a whole.

The precise constitutional balance between individual ownership and shared stewardship remains an open area for future development.

---

## Summary

| Principle | Implication |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Planning visibility** | Conflicts identified before resources are committed |
| **Cooperation first** | Joint ventures preferred over competition |
| **Contribution recognition** | Provenance preserves incentive for initiative |
| **Information stewardship** | Authorship persists beyond participation |
| **Re-entry consistency** | Former participants use normal public processes |
| **Anti-abuse safeguards** | Shared resources protected from exploitation |
| **Stewardship focus** | Assets treated as civilisational resources, not absolute property |
