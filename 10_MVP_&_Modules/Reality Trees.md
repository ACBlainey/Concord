# Reality Trees: A Portable Cognitive and Experimental Exploration Module

**Alexander C Blainey**

**2026**

---

## Abstract

Reality Trees constitute a structured method for exploring multiple possible pathways, hypotheses, or explanations arising from a present state. Rather than requiring immediate selection of a single assumed answer, the method allows alternative possibilities to be identified, examined, tested, and progressively refined. The objective is not to predict the future with certainty, but to make possible consequences visible, reduce premature commitment, and provide a structure for investigation. Reality Trees function as both a personal cognitive tool for managing uncertainty and a framework for civilisational experimentation, bridging individual belief structures with formal hypothesis testing and real-world branching.

---

## 1. Overview and Foundational Principles

A Reality Tree is a symbolic visualisation of possible realities, hypotheses, or pathways arising from a present state. Each branch represents a possibility and may be assigned a **confidence weighting**—not necessarily representing mathematical probability, but rather the degree to which the possibility is currently believed, supported, or considered plausible.

In its simplest form, a Reality Tree represents:

> **"I don't know. I am torn between these possibilities."**

The concept originated from the author's own perception methodology as a way of visualising personal beliefs and uncertainty. Reality Trees form part of the broader Blaineyan Reasoning approach to moving between hypotheses, abstraction levels, and possible outcomes.

**Reality remains the final arbiter.**

---

## 2. The Basic Method

Starting from an observed situation:

1. **Identify the present state.**
2. **Generate possible explanations, consequences, or pathways.**
3. **Branch where meaningful alternatives exist.**
4. **Examine the assumptions and causal relationships behind each branch.**
5. **Assess evidence, uncertainty, benefits, harms, and possible failure modes.**
6. **Test promising branches where practical.**
7. **Retain the results, including unsuccessful branches.**
8. **Refine the model as new evidence becomes available.**

A branch is therefore not automatically a conclusion. It is a possibility to be investigated.

---

## 3. Cognitive Function and Personal Application

### 3.1 Separating Belief from Certainty

At the personal level, Reality Trees provide a way of separating **belief from certainty**. Holding a belief as an absolute reality can create cognitive dissonance when that belief is challenged by contradictory evidence. A Reality Tree allows the individual to retain a preferred interpretation while acknowledging alternative possibilities.

The mind can therefore move between branches when considering different perspectives without necessarily abandoning the original branch. A person can effectively say:

**"This is what I currently believe, but I recognise these other possibilities and can examine them."**

This creates a structured form of intellectual flexibility.

### 3.2 Opposing Possibilities

Reality Trees are particularly useful when apparently opposing beliefs may both be potentially true, while confidence in each varies. Instead of forcing a person to select one belief as *true* and reject the other as *false*, the alternatives can remain simultaneously represented:

**Possibility A — 70% confidence**  
**Possibility B — 30% confidence**

The exact weighting can change as evidence, reasoning, or discussion develops. This allows uncertainty to be represented explicitly rather than hidden behind a premature conclusion.

### 3.3 Devil's-Advocate Branches

A Reality Tree does not require a branch to be believed. A branch may be:

- strongly believed;
- weakly believed;
- uncertain;
- considered plausible but unsupported;
- not believed at all;
- or actively dismissed.

Even a dismissed possibility can remain useful as a **devil's-advocate branch**. Its purpose is to ask:

**"If this were true, what would follow?"**

This allows ideas to be explored without confusing exploration with endorsement.

---

## 4. From Belief to Hypothesis

Reality Trees provide a bridge between personal belief and formal hypothesis. A branch does not have to be accepted as reality in order to be examined. It can simply represent a possible explanation or pathway whose consequences are worth exploring.

The tree makes alternative possibilities visible while preserving their differing levels of confidence.

When combined with other reasoning methods within the broader Blaineyan Reasoning framework, Reality Trees become particularly useful for:

- exposing hidden assumptions;
- exploring competing explanations;
- examining failure modes;
- moving between abstraction levels;
- considering consequences;
- resisting premature conclusions;
- identifying useful research questions;
- and progressively pruning unsupported branches.

The underlying principle is not that every branch is equally valid. It is that **possibilities can be explored before they are accepted or rejected**.

---

## 5. From Cognition to Civilisation: The Experimental Hierarchy

The same principle extends beyond individual reasoning into a hierarchy of experimentation:

**Speculation → Simulation → Limited Pilot → Real-World Branch → Knowledge Integration**

At the earliest level, a branch is simply an imagined possibility. At later levels it can become an experiment conducted under controlled conditions. Early branches can therefore be explored at relatively low cost before greater resources or irreversible commitments are made.

Evidence generated by one branch can then inform the wider system. This creates a connection between cognitive exploration and civilisational experimentation.

---

## 6. Civilisational Branching

When applied to civilisation, branches may represent:

- **Experimental branches** — competing solutions tested independently.
- **Environmental branches** — adaptations to different environments such as Earth, Mars, or orbital habitats.
- **Compatibility branches** — peaceful separation where genuine incompatibility cannot presently be reconciled.

Different branches can therefore generate evidence without requiring an entire civilisation to commit to one untested solution simultaneously.

### 6.1 Failure as Information

A central principle is that failure in one branch does not necessarily constitute failure of the wider system. Where branches are appropriately isolated, an unsuccessful experiment can become **local learning rather than global catastrophe**. Successful approaches can be adopted elsewhere, while unsuccessful approaches provide information about what does not work.

This makes controlled diversity a potential source of civilisational resilience.

---

## 7. Shared Knowledge and Documentation

Where Reality Trees develop into real-world branches, branches should preserve and, where practical, share:

- provenance;
- assumptions and methodology;
- reasoning and experimental rationale;
- confidence assessments;
- measured outcomes;
- unintended consequences;
- successful discoveries;
- unsuccessful discoveries;
- and lessons learned.

The purpose of branching is therefore not to create isolated knowledge silos, but to allow multiple paths to generate evidence that can improve the wider system.

---

## 8. Status and Scope

**Proposed portable module.**

Reality Trees should not be understood as a method for determining which reality is true by visualisation alone. They are a means of making uncertainty, competing possibilities, and alternative hypotheses explicit.

Reality Trees are not presented as a claim that every possible future can be predicted. They are a framework for making alternative possibilities visible, reducing premature commitment, and generating evidence through progressively stronger forms of testing.

Their value lies in allowing possibilities to be **held, weighted, explored, challenged, and eventually refined or discarded without premature commitment**.

Within Blaineyan Reasoning, Reality Trees provide a visual structure for the exploration of the unknown.

**Reality remains the final arbiter.**
