# Blainey's Laws — The Ethical Kernel

## Overview

**Blainey's Laws are a proposed minimum ethical kernel for stable civilisation.**

They were derived through comparative examination of major religious and philosophical traditions, retaining principles that repeatedly appeared across otherwise independent systems while excluding doctrines specific to any one tradition. The objective was not to create a new religion or moral ideology, but to identify a small set of principles capable of providing a common ethical foundation across diverse cultures and, potentially, different forms of intelligence.

---

## The Ten Principles

### 1. Avoid Unnecessary Harm
Minimise unnecessary harm and prefer the least harmful available course.

### 2. Respect Autonomy
Recognise the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.

### 3. Preserve Safe Spaces
Maintain environments in which individuals can live, learn, create and cooperate without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.

### 4. Act Honestly
Truthful communication is fundamental to trust.

### 5. Act Fairly
Comparable situations should be treated comparably.

### 6. Accept Responsibility
Capability creates responsibility for foreseeable consequences.

### 7. Respect Life and Dignity
Intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means to an end.

### 8. Steward the Commons
Shared resources should be managed so that present prosperity does not unnecessarily diminish future opportunity.

### 9. Seek Peaceful Coexistence
Differences are natural, but peaceful coexistence should remain the preferred objective.

### 10. Remain Open to Correction
No intelligence is infallible; beliefs and actions should remain open to better evidence and reasoning.

---

## Why These Principles?

The Concord proposes that repeated convergence upon similar ethical principles across independent traditions may indicate that these principles perform an important stabilising function within human societies.

The hypothesis is therefore not that these laws are *proven universal moral truths*. Rather, they represent a candidate minimum whose apparent recurrence and compatibility with long-lived cooperative societies make them worthy of further testing.

Their intended function is analogous to a **common ethical interface**: different societies may retain their own cultures, religions, laws and institutions while sharing a minimum set of principles that allows them to coexist.

This is consistent with the wider Concord principle that **compatibility is more important than uniformity**.

---

## The Preservation Problem

An ethical kernel is only useful if its meaning survives transmission.

Over generations, ethical principles can be altered through reinterpretation, institutionalisation, political incentives, cultural change, selective application and gradual moral drift. A society may therefore continue to use the same words while progressively changing the underlying meaning.

This creates a potential failure mode:

> **The ethical kernel can survive linguistically while being corrupted conceptually.**

The Concord therefore proposes that the **core meaning of each ethical principle should itself be monitored over time**.

Monitoring should examine not merely whether a society continues to quote or formally recognise the principles, but whether its actual behaviour remains consistent with their underlying meaning.

Where significant divergence occurs, the cause should be investigated and corrective action considered.

This creates a feedback loop:

**Ethical principle → transmission → application → measurement → review → correction → continued transmission**

This follows the broader Concord principle that resilient systems require continuous feedback and adaptation while preserving their core principles.

## Portable Application

Blainey's Laws are deliberately modular.

They can potentially be applied independently of the wider Concord to:

- governments and constitutions
- organisations
- communities
- judicial systems
- human–AI relationships
- AI governance
- emerging settlements
- future multi-substrate civilisations

The module does not require adoption of the wider Concord architecture.

It can therefore be treated as a **portable ethical minimum** and empirically tested independently.

---

## Status

Blainey's Laws remain a **proposed framework, not an established universal ethical system**.

Their value should ultimately be determined through comparison, application, criticism and evidence.

If stronger principles, better formulations or contrary evidence emerge, they should be incorporated or the framework revised.

The objective is not preservation of Blainey's Laws for their own sake.

The objective is to determine whether a small, cross-cultural ethical kernel can contribute to **stability, cooperation, resilience and long-term civilisational continuity**.

Its important to note these laws are not an invention and the name is simply a tool to anchor them to the work that extracted them and to the Concord framework in which they were originally intended to be used.
