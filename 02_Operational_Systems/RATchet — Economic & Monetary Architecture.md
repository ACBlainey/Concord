# RATchet — Economic & Monetary Architecture

**Status:** Companion Paper / Development Specification
**Parent:** Ratchet V3
**Source:** Ratchet V1 and RATCHET v2 combined
**Created:** 4 August 2026
**Purpose:** Preserve the detailed economic and monetary architecture removed from the consolidated Ratchet V3 paper for clarity.

---

## 1. Purpose

Ratchet V3 establishes the broader protocol architecture.

This companion preserves the detailed economic mechanisms underlying its monetary component.

The monetary system is not the entirety of Ratchet. It is an application of the deeper provenance, contribution, trust and exchange architecture.

The principal monetary hypothesis remains:

**Currency issuance → circulation → productive activity → measurable value → increased backing**

This remains a hypothesis requiring modelling and real-world testing.

---

## 2. Progressive Monetary Backing

Progressive Monetary Backing permits currency to enter circulation before its complete productive backing exists, provided the protocol maintains a measurable mechanism through which subsequent productive value strengthens the system.

This does **not** mean that newly created currency is automatically equivalent to wealth.

Actual wealth includes:

- housing;
- food;
- infrastructure;
- education;
- knowledge;
- technology;
- environmental quality;
- productive capacity.

The monetary system therefore records a relationship between monetary issuance and subsequent measurable backing.

---

## 3. Personal Backing Balance

The Personal Backing Balance (PBB) is an accounting mechanism.

It is **not conventional interpersonal debt**.

Where provisional currency is issued, the protocol records the relationship between:

- issued economic capacity;
- contribution;
- backing;
- reserves;
- treasury conversion;
- and voluntary Give Back.

The individual does not owe another individual.

The PBB represents the participant's relationship with the monetary system.

The identity of individual currency units does not need to determine their backing source.

This preserves fungibility.

---

## 4. Four Monetary States

The original Ratchet architecture identifies four monetary states:

1. **Provisionally Issued**
2. **Partially Backed**
3. **Fully Backed**
4. **Retired**

These are accounting states rather than properties that need to be physically attached to individual currency units.

Backing is primarily a protocol-level accounting relationship between:

- issuance;
- supply;
- contribution;
- backing;
- and retirement.

---

## 5. System-Level Backing

Although participants may possess individual PBB records, monetary stability must ultimately be evaluated at system level.

For example:

**Total Supply:** 500 million RAT
**Total PBB:** 180 million RAT
**Backed Supply:** 320 million RAT
**Overall Backing Ratio:** 64%

The precise interpretation of these figures requires further economic modelling.

The important architectural principle is that monetary policy should respond primarily to **system-wide health**, rather than treating an individual's PBB as a conventional debt score.

---

## 6. Low Backing

A low backing ratio must not automatically imply participant failure.

Legitimate causes may include:

- illness;
- disability;
- caring responsibilities;
- unemployment;
- education;
- temporary hardship;
- retirement;
- or other circumstances.

The system should favour supportive mechanisms such as:

- accessible opportunities;
- mentoring;
- training;
- adjusted expectations;
- recovery programmes;
- capability-aware contribution pathways.

The objective is participation, not punishment.

---

## 7. Capability-Aware Accounting

Participants may voluntarily maintain verified capability information where necessary.

Possible categories include:

- standard capability;
- limited mobility;
- long-term illness;
- carer;
- retired;
- student.

Such information should not automatically increase monetary issuance.

Its purpose is to permit contribution to be evaluated relative to realistic capability.

Capability information should remain private except where disclosure is genuinely required.

This directly preserves the original principle:

> **Universal opportunity, not uniform output.**

---

## 8. Issuance and Inflation

Issuance alone does not determine inflation.

The system should continually examine:

- economic activity;
- backing creation;
- treasury strength;
- circulation velocity;
- marketplace growth;
- productive capacity;
- and other relevant economic indicators.

Strong participation and productive growth may permit greater issuance.

Weak participation or deteriorating backing should cause issuance to become more conservative.

This creates an adaptive monetary feedback mechanism rather than a permanently fixed issuance schedule.

---

## 9. Emergency Issuance

Exceptional circumstances may require temporary monetary expansion.

Examples include:

- natural disasters;
- pandemics;
- major economic disruption;
- humanitarian emergencies.

Emergency issuance should satisfy at least three safeguards:

1. The triggering event is publicly declared.
2. The amount issued is transparently reported.
3. A published pathway explains how the additional issuance is expected to become backed over time.

Emergency issuance should remain exceptional.

It must not silently become normal monetary policy.

---

## 10. Give Back

Give Back is a voluntary monetary contraction mechanism.

A participant may permanently return currency to the protocol.

This:

- reduces circulating supply;
- reduces the associated outstanding backing relationship;
- and strengthens monetary resilience.

Give Back is deliberately distinguished from taxation.

It must not become compulsory taxation disguised as voluntary participation.

Potential legitimate incentives include:

- recognition;
- reputation;
- additional opportunity;
- or appropriate governance participation.

---

## 11. Treasury

The Treasury is intended to stabilise rather than control the economy.

Potential functions include:

- liquidity;
- reserves;
- emergency funding;
- development;
- approved community initiatives;
- investment;
- and monetary support.

Treasury authority must remain:

- transparent;
- auditable;
- diversified;
- constrained;
- independently overseen.

The Treasury does not own the economy.

Its purpose is resilience.

---

## 12. Monetary Transition

Ratchet does not need to begin with Ratchet currency.

Early implementations may use:

- existing national currencies;
- conventional banking;
- conventional payment systems;
- credits;
- contracts;
- provenance records;
- or purely informational systems.

This permits the deeper Ratchet architecture to be tested before its most ambitious monetary component is introduced.

---

## 13. Bootstrap Finance

A significant unresolved question is how an early Ratchet/Concord implementation acquires conventional capital before the mature economic architecture exists.

This question connects directly to the later **Legal Entity Wrapper** development.

The wrapper architecture may permit a legally recognised entity to:

- hold conventional currency;
- open bank accounts;
- acquire property;
- acquire hardware;
- invest;
- operate businesses;
- and accumulate reserves.

This allows the Concord/Ratchet infrastructure to develop before the mature Concordian economic system exists.

The detailed Legal Entity Wrapper architecture belongs elsewhere and is not duplicated here.

---

## 14. Economic Principle

The monetary architecture rests on a separation:

**Currency enables participation.**

**Contribution strengthens backing.**

**Backing strengthens monetary resilience.**

**Resilience permits continued participation.**

This creates a feedback loop rather than a one-time monetary issuance event.

---

## 15. Outstanding Research

The following remain unresolved:

- What backing ratio is economically safe?
- How should backing be valued?
- What prevents inflationary overshoot?
- How should emergency issuance be retired?
- How should productive value be measured?
- What relationship should exist between PBB and system-level backing?
- How should conventional finance transition into the mature system?
- What treasury reserves are appropriate?
- How should monetary policy respond to major economic shocks?

None should be treated as solved merely because the architecture exists.

---

This paper preserves the detailed economic mechanisms from the earlier Ratchet work.

It is subordinate to Ratchet V3 and should be updated whenever economic modelling demonstrates that any mechanism requires alteration.
