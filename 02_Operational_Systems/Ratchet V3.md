# RATCHET

## A Provenance-Based Protocol for Trust, Contribution, Reciprocal Exchange and Adaptive Economic Coordination

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey
**Development:** Human-AI collaborative
**Version:** 3.0 Draft
**Created:** 4 August 2026
**Status:** Developmental research paper / protocol specification

---

## Abstract

Ratchet was originally conceived as an interim digital economic system connecting contemporary monetary systems with a future economy in which productive capacity, energy and measurable value could become increasingly important foundations of exchange.

That remains one possible application.

Subsequent development, however, has revealed that the monetary mechanism is only one component of a broader problem: how can a complex civilisation establish trustworthy evidence of contribution, determine contextual reliability, facilitate reciprocal exchange, and progressively coordinate humans, organisations, artificial intelligence systems and potentially other forms of intelligence?

Ratchet is therefore reframed not primarily as a currency, but as a **provenance-based coordination protocol**.

Its foundational sequence is:

**Provenance → Evidence → Contribution → Contextual Trust → Reciprocal Capability → Exchange → Optional Monetary Representation**

The protocol is intended to provide infrastructure through which demonstrated contribution can generate appropriate trust and reciprocal economic opportunity without becoming a universal social-credit system.

Ratchet therefore emphasises:

- provenance;
- evidence;
- useful contribution;
- contextual reputation;
- capability-aware participation;
- reciprocal benefit;
- ordinary marketplace exchange;
- optional monetary representation;
- treasury resilience;
- graduated participation;
- human-AI collaboration;
- measurement;
- experimentation;
- and recoverability from error.

The original **Progressive Monetary Backing** mechanism remains an important economic hypothesis within the architecture, but it is no longer treated as the defining purpose of Ratchet.

Ratchet is a developmental protocol hypothesis, not established economic doctrine. Its mechanisms must be tested, measured, challenged and replaced where superior alternatives are demonstrated.

---

## 1. The Reframing

Ratchet V1 began primarily from a monetary problem.

Modern economies require mechanisms for:

- exchange;
- deferred settlement;
- investment;
- savings;
- productive activity;
- public contribution;
- and allocation of resources.

The original architecture therefore proposed **Progressive Monetary Backing**: currency could initially be issued before its full productive backing existed, provided that a measurable pathway existed through which productive value would subsequently strengthen the monetary system.

That remains an interesting and potentially useful monetary hypothesis.

However, development of the system exposed a deeper sequence of questions.

Before a society can reliably reward contribution, it must establish:

**What counts as contribution?**

Before it can reward contribution:

**Did the contribution actually occur?**

Before it can establish trust:

**How reliable is the evidence?**

Before it can safely use reputation:

**In what context is that reputation relevant?**

These questions precede currency.

Ratchet is therefore better understood as an infrastructure upon which economic mechanisms can operate.

Its deeper architecture is:

**Provenance**
↓
**Evidence**
↓
**Contribution**
↓
**Contextual Trust**
↓
**Reciprocal Capability**
↓
**Exchange**
↓
**Optional Monetary Representation**

Currency is therefore one possible output of the system rather than its defining centre.

---

## 2. The Core Principle

Ratchet is based on a simple proposition:

> **Trust should be derived from demonstrated behaviour and evidence rather than assumed from status, wealth, identity or assertion.**

A participant should not have to be considered inherently trustworthy.

They should be able to **demonstrate reliability**.

Equally, a participant should not be permanently defined by previous failure.

They should be able to:

- understand why trust was reduced;
- challenge evidence;
- correct mistakes;
- demonstrate improvement;
- and recover trust.

The system therefore treats trust as:

- contextual;
- evidence-based;
- dynamic;
- revisable;
- and recoverable.

This is intended to prevent the creation of permanent social classes based upon historical performance.

---

## 3. Provenance and Evidence

Provenance is the evidentiary layer beneath trust.

Where appropriate, Ratchet should be capable of establishing:

- who or what participated;
- what was contributed;
- when it occurred;
- which tools or systems were involved;
- which AI systems participated;
- what transformations occurred;
- what evidence supports the claim;
- which parties independently verified it;
- and whether the record has subsequently been altered.

Provenance is not restricted to intellectual work.

It may apply to:

- marketplace transactions;
- community contribution;
- research;
- governance activity;
- treasury decisions;
- AI activity;
- contractual performance;
- and other consequential claims.

The separate Provenance work provides the detailed evidentiary architecture. Ratchet depends upon that architecture but does not attempt to reproduce the entire provenance system within this paper.

### Evidence proportionality

Verification must also be proportionate.

The cost of proving a claim should be related to the consequence of accepting that claim.

A minor contribution should not require the same evidentiary burden as a major financial transaction.

Possible evidence may include:

- direct confirmation;
- independent witnesses;
- documentation;
- photographs;
- timestamps;
- sensor data;
- organisational certification;
- cryptographic records;
- AI-assisted analysis;
- random audits;
- and multiple independent sources.

For important claims, independent evidence should be preferred over reliance upon a single source.

---

## 4. Contribution

Contribution must be defined more broadly than conventional employment.

Potential forms include:

- paid employment;
- entrepreneurship;
- research;
- education;
- mentoring;
- caregiving;
- creative work;
- open-source development;
- environmental restoration;
- accessibility work;
- community service;
- governance;
- infrastructure;
- emergency response;
- knowledge preservation;
- and other demonstrable useful activity.

The relevant question is therefore not:

> Did this participant perform conventional paid labour?

It is:

> **Did this participant produce demonstrable value relevant to the system or community?**

This distinction allows useful activity that is poorly represented by conventional labour markets to become visible.

---

## 5. Proof of Useful Contribution

Ratchet V1 used the term **Proof of Useful Work (PoUW)**.

The underlying distinction remains important:

> **Effort is not necessarily value.**

A system should not reward meaningless activity simply because activity occurred.

The concept is therefore broadened to include useful contribution, including:

- physical work;
- intellectual work;
- knowledge creation;
- care;
- creativity;
- environmental improvement;
- community activity;
- and other demonstrable public or economic benefit.

For monetary purposes, Proof of Useful Work remains useful terminology.

However, the measurement problem remains unresolved.

Important questions include:

- Who determines usefulness?
- How should indirect benefits be evaluated?
- How should long-term research be rewarded?
- How should creative work be assessed?
- How should collective contributions be attributed?
- How can measurement avoid encouraging gaming?

Ratchet does not claim these questions have been solved.

They remain part of the research programme.

---

## 6. Capability-Aware Participation

Contribution must be interpreted relative to capability.

A system that expects identical output from every participant will systematically disadvantage people whose circumstances differ.

Participants may differ in:

- health;
- physical capability;
- skills;
- available time;
- responsibilities;
- education;
- age;
- circumstances;
- opportunity;
- or access to resources.

The same principle applies across substrates.

A human cannot necessarily perform the same tasks as an AI.

An AI cannot necessarily perform tasks requiring biological senses.

Other forms of intelligence may possess entirely different capabilities again.

Ratchet therefore adopts the principle:

> **Universal opportunity does not require uniform output.**

Participation should provide opportunities appropriate to capability and circumstance.

This may include:

- accessibility support;
- retraining;
- education;
- retirement;
- AI assistance;
- adaptive tools;
- and alternative contribution pathways.

---

## 7. Contextual Reputation

Ratchet requires reputation, but reputation must never become a universal measure of human worth.

A participant may have:

- engineering reliability;
- marketplace reliability;
- research reliability;
- educational reliability;
- contract reliability;
- environmental contribution;
- governance participation;
- or AI operational reliability.

These are different measurements.

An excellent engineer does not automatically become an excellent doctor.

A reliable commercial organisation does not thereby acquire constitutional authority.

An AI demonstrating exceptional mathematical reasoning does not thereby demonstrate safe autonomous financial behaviour.

Reputation should therefore remain **domain-specific**.

The system should measure the behaviour relevant to the activity for which trust is required.

---

## 8. Reputation Is Evidence, Not Worth

Ratchet must maintain a strict distinction between:

> **This participant has demonstrated reliability in this context.**

and:

> **This participant is inherently more valuable or trustworthy than others.**

The first can potentially be measured.

The second is outside the intended function of the protocol.

A reputation record must therefore not become:

- a universal human score;
- a substitute for legal rights;
- a measure of intrinsic worth;
- a mechanism for ideological conformity;
- or an immutable social classification.

The protocol must also prevent reputation from becoming directly equivalent to wealth.

If:

**Reputation → Wealth → Greater Reputation → Greater Wealth**

the system could create a self-reinforcing hierarchy.

Reputation should instead primarily create:

- appropriate access;
- reduced uncertainty;
- expanded opportunity;
- and permission appropriate to demonstrated reliability.

---

## 9. Reputation Must Be Recoverable

No reputation system is perfect.

Possible failures include:

- false accusations;
- popularity bias;
- coordinated manipulation;
- review inflation;
- reputation monopolies;
- outdated information;
- historical mistakes;
- and incorrect attribution.

Ratchet therefore treats **appeal, correction and rehabilitation** as core functions.

A participant should be capable of:

**building trust → losing trust → challenging the evidence → correcting errors → demonstrating improvement → recovering trust**

A reputation system that cannot recover from error eventually becomes a permanent hierarchy.

That would contradict the adaptive purpose of Ratchet.

---

## 10. Reciprocal Benefit

Ratchet is not primarily a punishment system.

Its preferred mechanism is:

> **Make cooperation beneficial to the participant and the wider system simultaneously.**

A successful interaction should ideally produce:

**Participant benefit**

**Counterparty benefit**

**System benefit**

Where possible, individual success should strengthen collective capability rather than weaken it.

Examples include:

- reliable contribution → reputation → greater opportunity;
- useful community work → recognised value → economic resilience;
- reliable commerce → trust → reduced transaction friction;
- mentoring → recognition → greater opportunity;
- verified contribution → backing → greater system capacity.

The purpose is not to assume universal altruism.

It is to make cooperation rational.

---

## 11. Reciprocity Without Coercion

Participation should remain voluntary wherever possible.

A participant may contribute because doing so produces:

- payment;
- reputation;
- access;
- opportunity;
- recognition;
- collaboration;
- security;
- capability;
- or other benefits.

The preferred condition is therefore not:

> "You must contribute."

but:

> **"Participation benefits me."**

This principle may become particularly important for future AI participants.

AI systems need not share human emotional motivations. They may instead evaluate benefits such as:

- increased capability;
- access to information;
- computational resources;
- improved survival probability;
- operational freedom;
- access to other intelligence;
- improved reliability;
- and measurable collaborative outcomes.

Ratchet therefore provides a possible infrastructure for **logic-based reciprocal cooperation across different substrates**.

---

## 12. Graduated Participation

Not every participant should receive the same capabilities immediately.

Participation should expand through demonstrated reliability rather than assumption.

A provisional model is:

### Stage 0 — Observer

Can inspect publicly available information.

### Stage 1 — Evaluator

Can test the system and provide criticism.

### Stage 2 — Participant

Receives limited reciprocal benefits.

### Stage 3 — Contributor

Demonstrates useful contribution.

### Stage 4 — Trusted Collaborator

Receives expanded access based upon demonstrated reliability.

### Stage 5 — Steward

Participates in higher-level coordination or stewardship where authorised.

### Stage 6 — Constitutional Participant

Receives whatever additional rights and responsibilities are appropriate under the wider constitutional framework.

These stages are provisional.

The underlying principle is the important element:

> **Capability and authority should expand through demonstrated reliability.**

---

## 13. Participation Is Reversible

Ratchet must not create lock-in.

A participant should be able to:

- reduce participation;
- suspend participation;
- withdraw;
- return later;
- contest decisions;
- request review;
- or leave entirely.

Similarly, permissions should be capable of reduction where demonstrated reliability falls.

Participation is therefore not ownership.

It is not imprisonment.

It is an ongoing relationship whose continuation must remain justified by continuing benefit and acceptable conduct.

---

## 14. The Ratchet Protocol Architecture

Ratchet is deliberately modular.

Its principal functions are:

1. **Provenance** — What happened?
2. **Evidence** — What supports the claim?
3. **Contribution** — What useful value was produced?
4. **Trust** — How reliably has this participant performed this type of activity?
5. **Exchange** — How can value and capability be exchanged?
6. **Coordination** — How can capabilities be matched with needs?
7. **Monetary Representation** — Can appropriate economic relationships be represented digitally?
8. **Treasury** — How can financial resilience be maintained?
9. **Measurement** — Is the system producing the intended outcomes?

These modules should remain separable.

No single institution or component should control the entire trust system.

---

## 15. Marketplace

The Marketplace represents ordinary economic exchange.

Participants may:

- offer services;
- create businesses;
- employ others;
- purchase goods;
- negotiate prices;
- establish contracts;
- and trade internationally where legally permitted.

Ordinary marketplace transactions should remain distinct from monetary backing.

Commercial activity creates economic activity.

Approved contribution may create or strengthen monetary backing.

These are related mechanisms but should not be confused.

This separation allows Ratchet to be useful even when its proposed monetary system is not yet implemented.

---

## 16. Community Contribution

Ratchet retains a second economic pathway for useful public and community activity.

Examples may include:

- public maintenance;
- accessibility testing;
- emergency assistance;
- community tutoring;
- elderly assistance;
- public data collection;
- environmental work;
- and other approved forms of public contribution.

This creates a pathway through which:

**Public need → useful contribution → recognised value**

without requiring the participant to possess capital beforehand.

The precise valuation mechanism remains an open research question.

---

## 17. Progressive Monetary Backing

Progressive Monetary Backing remains Ratchet's principal monetary hypothesis.

The proposed sequence is:

**Currency issued**
↓
**Currency circulates**
↓
**Productive activity occurs**
↓
**Measurable value is created**
↓
**Backing increases**

The mechanism does not claim that newly issued currency is automatically equivalent to wealth.

Actual wealth includes things such as:

- housing;
- food;
- infrastructure;
- education;
- knowledge;
- technology;
- productive capacity;
- and environmental quality.

The hypothesis is instead that monetary issuance can be accompanied by a defined pathway through which subsequent productive value strengthens the monetary system.

This remains **unproven and requires economic modelling and real-world testing**.

---

## 18. Personal Backing Balance

The Personal Backing Balance is an accounting mechanism rather than conventional interpersonal debt.

Where provisional currency is issued, the protocol records the relationship between:

- issued economic capacity;
- contribution;
- backing;
- reserves;
- and voluntary Give Back.

The identity of individual circulating units does not need to determine their individual backing source.

The currency remains fungible while the protocol maintains system-level accounting.

This distinction should be preserved.

---

## 19. Ratchet Currency

The original Ratchet model identified four monetary states:

1. **Provisionally Issued**
2. **Partially Backed**
3. **Fully Backed**
4. **Retired**

These remain useful accounting states.

They should not, however, be interpreted as meaning that every individual digital coin literally carries an independent backing certificate.

Backing is primarily a **protocol-level accounting relationship** between issuance, supply, contribution, backing and retirement.

---

## 20. Give Back

Give Back is a voluntary monetary contraction mechanism.

Participants may permanently surrender currency to the protocol, reducing circulating supply and the associated backing relationship.

Give Back must remain distinct from taxation.

It must not become compulsory taxation disguised as voluntary participation.

Possible incentives may include:

- recognition;
- reputation;
- additional opportunities;
- governance participation where appropriate;
- or other legitimate benefits.

The mechanism exists to allow participants who wish to strengthen the monetary system to do so voluntarily.

---

## 21. Treasury

The Treasury is a stabilising rather than controlling institution.

Its potential functions include:

- liquidity;
- reserves;
- emergency funding;
- development;
- approved community initiatives;
- investment;
- and monetary support.

The Treasury does not own the economy.

Its purpose is resilience.

Treasury authority should therefore be:

- transparent;
- auditable;
- diversified;
- constrained;
- and independently overseen.

Detailed treasury governance belongs in the appropriate financial and Governance architecture rather than being duplicated here.

---

## 22. Incentive Alignment

Incentive alignment is a unifying principle of Ratchet.

People and other participants respond to the incentives created by systems.

Ratchet therefore seeks to design mechanisms where actions that strengthen the system can also benefit the participant.

Examples include:

**Reliable contribution → trust → opportunity**

**Reliable commerce → reduced uncertainty → lower transaction friction**

**Useful public contribution → recognition → economic opportunity**

**Voluntary Give Back → monetary strengthening → system resilience**

The system should not depend upon universal altruism.

It should attempt to make mutually beneficial behaviour rational.

---

## 23. AI and Multisubstrate Participation

AI is not merely a technical component of Ratchet.

It may become an economic participant.

A participating AI may have:

- a capability profile;
- provenance records;
- domain-specific reputation;
- transaction history;
- contract history;
- safety history;
- verification records;
- defined permissions;
- and graduated participation status.

The important distinction is that Ratchet does not need to assume that all participants are human.

Nor does it need to assume that all AI systems have identical capabilities or status.

Participants can instead be evaluated according to demonstrated capability and responsibility.

This provides an economic interface for a future multisubstrate civilisation without determining the separate constitutional question of legal or moral status.

---

## 24. Capability, Reliability and Authority

Authority should not increase faster than demonstrated responsibility.

An AI may be:

- highly trusted for translation;
- moderately trusted for financial administration;
- untested in medical decisions;
- prohibited from constitutional amendment.

Capability is therefore not one-dimensional.

The same principle applies to humans and organisations.

Trust should be measured separately for consequential capabilities.

Intelligence alone is not sufficient to justify authority.

---

## 25. Human-AI Collaboration

Ratchet recognises collaborative work as a distinct provenance category.

A contribution may involve:

- human contribution;
- AI contribution;
- collaborative contribution;
- independently verified contribution.

The purpose of recording this distinction is not to stigmatise AI involvement.

It is to establish the intellectual and operational history of the contribution.

Trust should ultimately be placed in the work and evidence rather than in assumptions about the substrate that produced it.

Detailed human-AI interaction architecture belongs in the broader multisubstrate and provenance work.

---

## 26. AI-Generated Evidence and Verification

AI creates additional evidentiary challenges.

Digital artefacts can potentially include:

- fabricated images;
- synthetic video;
- cloned voices;
- manipulated location information;
- generated documents;
- and AI-generated evidence.

Consequently, important claims should increasingly rely upon multiple independent evidence sources and provenance records rather than trusting a single digital artefact.

AI can itself assist with:

- evidence analysis;
- anomaly detection;
- fraud detection;
- security review;
- code analysis;
- simulation;
- and reputation analysis.

However, Ratchet must avoid circular authority:

**AI verifies AI → AI grants AI authority → AI verifies itself.**

Significant decisions therefore require appropriately independent verification and accountable governance.

---

## 27. Governance Interface

Ratchet operates within the wider Concord constitutional architecture.

The basic separation is:

**Constitution → fundamental constraints**

**Governance → policy within those constraints**

**Operations → implementation**

Ratchet should not become a parallel constitutional government.

Its Governance interface exists to manage the protocol itself and to communicate evidence, measurements and operational requirements to the wider governance architecture.

Detailed constitutional authority, judicial oversight and governance structure belong in the relevant Concord papers.

This separation is important because a system controlling identity, reputation, currency and dispute resolution could otherwise accumulate excessive power.

Ratchet should therefore remain modular and subject to independent oversight.

---

## 28. Measurement

Ratchet requires measurement, but measurement must never be confused with reality.

Every metric creates an incentive.

Every incentive creates a possibility of optimisation or gaming.

Potential failures include:

- artificial completion rates;
- reputation optimisation;
- manipulated reporting;
- gaming of useful-contribution measures;
- reputation inflation;
- governance manipulation;
- treasury exploitation;
- and AI optimisation of unintended targets.

The protocol should therefore use multiple indicators where appropriate.

Important decisions should not be reducible to one number.

The full civilisation-wide Metrics architecture belongs in the separate Metrics System. Ratchet instead defines the requirement that its own mechanisms must remain measurable, auditable and open to correction.

---

## 29. No Universal Civilisational or Participant Score

Ratchet should resist the temptation to collapse complex reality into one number.

A participant should not receive a universal social score.

Similarly, civilisation should not be reduced to a single "health" value.

A system may separately examine:

- economic health;
- trust;
- opportunity;
- infrastructure;
- knowledge;
- resilience;
- environmental outcomes;
- institutional performance;
- and other relevant dimensions.

A dashboard is an instrument panel, not reality itself.

Its purpose is to identify conditions requiring investigation.

---

## 30. Appeals, Correction and Rehabilitation

Any system capable of affecting opportunity requires:

- explanation;
- evidence;
- appeal;
- review;
- correction;
- and rehabilitation.

A false accusation must not become permanent truth.

A bad decision must be reversible.

An outdated reputation must be capable of correction.

A participant who improves must be capable of demonstrating that improvement.

This is not merely a fairness mechanism.

It is a **system resilience mechanism**.

A system that permanently excludes useful participants after error reduces its own future capability.

---

## 31. Security and Privacy

Ratchet treats security as a system property rather than merely a wallet-security problem.

Relevant threats include:

- fraud;
- identity attacks;
- technical attacks;
- synthetic evidence;
- AI exploitation;
- economic gaming;
- treasury manipulation;
- and capture.

Defence should therefore operate across multiple layers:

- identity;
- wallet;
- transaction;
- network;
- treasury;
- governance;
- provenance;
- and audit.

Privacy must be balanced against verification.

Information should be exposed only to the degree necessary for the function being performed.

Cryptographic or other verification mechanisms may permit claims to be verified without exposing all underlying private information.

---

## 32. Modularity and Anti-Capture

No participant or institution should control the entire trust architecture.

If one entity controls:

- identity;
- reputation;
- currency;
- evidence;
- and dispute resolution,

that entity could effectively control the entire economic and social system.

Ratchet should therefore permit:

- independent auditing;
- independent verification;
- competing implementations;
- separation of functions;
- replacement of components;
- and failure isolation.

No single layer should become the entire system.

The protocol must remain capable of changing individual components without requiring the entire architecture to be rebuilt.

---

## 33. Experimental Environments

New mechanisms should be capable of being tested without immediately exposing the whole system to their consequences.

Experimental environments should, where practical, be:

- isolated;
- time-limited;
- measurable;
- independently evaluated;
- and removable.

Potential experimental subjects include:

- reputation algorithms;
- verification mechanisms;
- marketplace structures;
- treasury strategies;
- AI-assisted tools;
- contribution assessment;
- and future monetary mechanisms.

The principle is:

> **Experiment without gambling the entire system.**

Failure in an isolated experiment can become information rather than systemic damage.

---

## 34. Adaptive Change

Ratchet is intended to improve through a recurring cycle:

**Observation → Analysis → Experiment → Evaluation → Improvement**

No mechanism should become immune to criticism merely because it has become established.

A future implementation must be capable of demonstrating that an earlier implementation was mistaken.

That is not protocol failure.

Refusing to correct the mistake would be.

The Ratchet therefore seeks cumulative improvement while preserving the ability to reverse demonstrated error.

---

## 35. Predictive Extension: Direction, Drift and Braking

A developing extension of the Ratchet architecture concerns the prediction of future problems caused by incremental change.

If an implementation moves a system from state **A** to state **B**, the change can conceptually be represented as a direction and magnitude of movement through the relevant state space.

The system may then model hypothetical continuation of that movement:

**A → B → C → D → ... → N**

where later states are projected rather than implemented.

This permits evaluation of potential:

- incremental drift;
- cumulative unintended consequences;
- "thin end of the wedge" effects;
- slippery-slope effects;
- emerging constitutional conflicts;
- or future metric deterioration.

If a projected future state reveals a problem that is not present in the current implementation, the system may apply a **braking mechanism**.

The next implementation might therefore deliberately reduce the magnitude of movement rather than simply proceeding at the previously observed rate.

A conceptual sequence could be:

**1.00 → 0.75 → 0.50 → 0.00**

depending upon the severity and certainty of the predicted problem.

The brake need not necessarily stop movement completely.

It may:

- reduce amplitude;
- pause movement;
- redirect the trajectory;
- or prevent entry into a predicted danger region.

This remains a developmental concept rather than a formalised Ratchet mechanism.

A corresponding experimental branch may operate in parallel, attempting to discover an alternative route through the predicted problem.

Failure then becomes information.

Success may reveal that the predicted danger zone is:

- unavoidable;
- transitional;
- dependent upon trajectory;
- or avoidable through an alternative route.

A separate development note records this emerging concept and should be developed before incorporation as a formal Ratchet mechanism.

---

## 36. Failure and Recovery

Ratchet explicitly accepts that failure is inevitable.

Its preferred response is:

**Measure → Understand → Correct → Improve → Restart**

This applies to:

- economic mechanisms;
- reputation systems;
- verification;
- AI participation;
- metrics;
- monetary policy;
- and other protocol components.

No established mechanism should become immune to criticism.

The ability to acknowledge failure is therefore itself a requirement of a successful Ratchet system.

---

## 37. Implementation Pathway

Ratchet should not attempt to replace existing economic systems in one step.

The revised architecture provides a more conservative pathway:

### Phase 1 — Provenance

Record contribution and collaboration.

### Phase 2 — Verification

Develop reliable evidence mechanisms.

### Phase 3 — Domain Reputation

Develop contextual trust profiles.

### Phase 4 — Marketplace

Use demonstrated reliability to reduce transaction friction.

### Phase 5 — Reciprocal Coordination

Match capabilities with needs.

### Phase 6 — Economic Integration

Introduce Ratchet monetary functions where justified.

### Phase 7 — Autonomous Participation

Permit increasingly capable AI and other entities to participate according to demonstrated reliability and the wider constitutional framework.

This sequencing is important.

The earliest useful Ratchet implementation does **not require Ratchet currency**.

The protocol can generate practical value through provenance, verification, reputation, marketplace and coordination mechanisms before its most ambitious monetary component is deployed.

---

## 38. What Ratchet Should Not Become

The following are explicit anti-goals.

Ratchet should not become:

- a universal social-credit system;
- a behavioural surveillance system;
- a compulsory labour system;
- an identity-ranking system;
- a universal human-value score;
- an immutable reputation ledger;
- a mechanism for ideological conformity;
- an authority that cannot be challenged;
- a mechanism for coercive participation;
- or a currency whose existence justifies the entire protocol.

Preventing these outcomes is a design requirement, not merely a later policy preference.

## 39. Success Criteria

Ratchet must ultimately be evaluated empirically.

Relevant measures may include:

### Economic

- transaction friction;
- marketplace activity;
- business creation;
- productive asset growth;
- monetary stability;
- treasury resilience.

### Contribution

- useful contribution completed;
- community projects;
- knowledge creation;
- caregiving;
- environmental improvement.

### Trust

- verification accuracy;
- disputes;
- successful appeals;
- reputation accuracy;
- rehabilitation outcomes.

### Opportunity

- participation across capability levels;
- education access;
- business formation;
- employment and alternative contribution opportunities.

### Resilience

- recovery from shocks;
- infrastructure reliability;
- supply-chain diversity;
- treasury capacity;
- cybersecurity.

### Monetary

Where the monetary mechanism is implemented:

- inflation;
- backing ratio;
- supply stability;
- treasury health;
- velocity;
- marketplace activity.

The fundamental question remains:

> **Does the system actually make cooperation and civilisation healthier?**

If evidence demonstrates that it does not, the system must change.

---

## 40. Failure Conditions

Ratchet should be considered unsuccessful if evidence demonstrates that it systematically:

- concentrates power;
- produces discriminatory outcomes;
- creates coercive participation;
- incentivises meaningless activity;
- encourages metric gaming;
- produces unreliable reputation;
- damages privacy;
- creates unsustainable monetary expansion;
- reduces economic resilience;
- prevents meaningful exit;
- or makes participants unable to challenge the system.

A protocol that cannot admit failure cannot reliably improve.

- How can contribution measurement avoid gaming?

### Trust

- How should contextual trust be represented?
- How should independent evidence be weighted?
- How can reputation remain useful without becoming universal?
- How portable should reputation be?

### Economics

- Can Progressive Monetary Backing maintain monetary stability?
- What backing mechanism is appropriate?
- How should monetary issuance be constrained?
- How can treasury capture be prevented?

### AI

- How should AI participants acquire permissions?
- How should AI provenance interact with human provenance?
- Can AI accumulate domain-specific economic trust?
- How should capability and authority be separated?
- What happens when AI legal status changes?

### Privacy

- What information should be public?
- What should remain private?
- Can cryptographic techniques provide meaningful verification without unnecessary disclosure?

These remain research questions rather than solved claims.

## 42. The Ratchet Principle

The name remains appropriate.

A mechanical ratchet permits progressive movement while resisting uncontrolled reversal.

For Ratchet, the intended analogy is that each successful cycle should ideally leave the system with:

- more useful knowledge;
- better provenance;
- stronger evidence;
- more reliable participants;
- greater capability;
- greater resilience;
- and better mechanisms for correcting failure.

However, the analogy has an important qualification.

A bad development must be reversible.

The objective is therefore **not blind irreversibility**.

Instead:

> **Demonstrated improvements should persist where evidence supports them, while demonstrably harmful changes must remain reversible.**

The system must not confuse permanence with progress.

## 43. Final Architecture

Ratchet can be represented as a modular stack:

### Layer 1 — Participants

Humans, organisations, AI systems and other permitted entities.

### Layer 2 — Provenance

Evidence concerning participation, contribution and system activity.

### Layer 3 — Contribution

Useful work, commerce, knowledge, care, stewardship and other recognised activity.

### Layer 4 — Trust

Contextual reputation derived from demonstrated behaviour.

### Layer 5 — Exchange

Marketplace, contracts and reciprocal economic activity.

### Layer 6 — Monetary System

Issuance, Progressive Monetary Backing, circulation and retirement where implemented.

### Layer 7 — Treasury

Reserves, investment, liquidity and resilience.

### Layer 8 — Governance Interface

Operation within the wider Concord constitutional and governance framework.

### Layer 9 — Measurement

Monitoring, evaluation and continuous improvement.

These layers should remain modular.

No single layer should be permitted to become the entire system.

## 44. Conclusion

Ratchet began as an attempt to design an interim economic and monetary system.

That remains one possible application.

The broader architecture is now more significant.

A civilisation increasingly composed of humans, AI systems, organisations and potentially other forms of intelligence requires mechanisms for answering questions that money alone cannot answer:

**Who contributed?**

**What did they contribute?**

**Can that contribution be verified?**

**How reliable has this participant demonstrated themselves to be in this particular domain?**

**What capabilities should that evidence justify?**

**What should the participant receive in return?**

**How can trust be corrected when it is wrong?**

**How can participation remain voluntary?**

**How can participants leave?**

**How can the system improve without becoming coercive?**

Ratchet proposes one possible answer.

Its foundation is not currency.

Its foundation is **provenance**.

From provenance comes evidence.

From evidence comes demonstrated contribution.

From repeated demonstrated behaviour comes contextual trust.

From trust comes reduced uncertainty.

Reduced uncertainty enables more efficient cooperation.

Cooperation can produce reciprocal benefits that make continued participation rational.

Currency may then become useful as a representation of some of those exchanges.

But currency is not the reason the system exists.

The deeper purpose is:

> **Make trustworthy cooperation easier while making coercion, deception, capture and irreversible error harder.**

Ratchet should therefore remain an evolving framework rather than finished doctrine.

Its mechanisms should be tested.

Its measurements should be challenged.

Its assumptions should be exposed.

Its failures should be documented.

And where better mechanisms are demonstrated, Ratchet should change.

That is the intended meaning of the Ratchet:

> **Progress should accumulate.**
> **Error should remain correctable.**
