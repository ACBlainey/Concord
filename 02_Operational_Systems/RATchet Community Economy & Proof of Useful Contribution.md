# RATchet — Community Economy & Proof of Useful Contribution

**Status:** Companion Paper / Development Specification
**Parent:** Ratchet V3
**Source:** Ratchet V1 and RATCHET v2 combined
**Created:** 4 August 2026

Alexander C Blainey

---

## 1. Purpose

Ratchet distinguishes between ordinary marketplace activity and protocol-recognised community contribution.

This companion preserves the operational detail of that distinction.

The purpose is not to replace ordinary markets with centrally planned work.

It is to create a second pathway through which genuine public value can become recognised economic contribution.

---

## 2. The Two Economies

### Marketplace Economy

The Marketplace:

- is created by participants;
- satisfies private demand;
- uses ordinary negotiated pricing;
- transfers existing currency;
- encourages competition;
- creates businesses;
- and generates ordinary economic wealth.

### Community Economy

The Community Economy:

- addresses collective needs;
- may be initiated by the protocol or authorised organisations;
- uses defined public-benefit criteria;
- can generate recognised contribution;
- and may strengthen monetary backing where the monetary system is active.

The distinction is fundamental.

**Marketplace activity creates ordinary economic value.**

**Community contribution can additionally create measurable public value and monetary backing.**

Community activity should complement markets rather than replace them.

---

## 3. Community Job Lifecycle

A basic community-job lifecycle is:

**Protocol / authorised organisation**

↓

**Community job created**

↓

**Participant accepts**

↓

**Work completed**

↓

**Verification**

↓

**Contribution recorded**

↓

**Backing relationship updated**

↓

**Community benefit realised**

The purpose is not simply to pay someone.

The work must produce a demonstrable external benefit.

## 5. Defining Useful Contribution

Useful contribution should produce one or more measurable forms of value:

- economic;
- environmental;
- educational;
- social;
- infrastructural;
- or resilience-related.

Activity performed solely to generate rewards without producing external value should not qualify.

The protocol rewards outcomes rather than mere activity.

---

## 6. Proof of Useful Work

Proof of Useful Work (PoUW) is the original Ratchet concept replacing the resource-consumption logic of conventional Proof of Work.

Traditional Proof of Work demonstrates that computational energy was consumed.

PoUW attempts to demonstrate that useful value was created.

Examples of weak PoUW include:

- meaningless clicks;
- repetitive artificial activity;
- manufactured tasks;
- or activity performed solely to generate rewards.

Examples of stronger contribution include:

- teaching;
- repairing infrastructure;
- environmental restoration;
- software development;
- caregiving;
- accessibility improvement.

The reward should relate to value produced rather than simply time spent.

---

## 7. Verification

Verification may use several levels.

### Direct Verification

An employer or requester confirms completion.

### Community Verification

Multiple independent participants confirm completion.

### Organisational Verification

An approved organisation verifies the contribution.

Examples include:

- charities;
- councils;
- schools;
- hospitals;
- recognised environmental organisations.

### AI Verification

AI may examine:

- photographs;
- GPS traces;
- timestamps;
- documents;
- sensor data;
- satellite imagery.

AI verification should assist rather than automatically replace accountable oversight.

### Hybrid Verification

The preferred scalable model may combine:

**Human judgement + AI assistance + random auditing**

---

## 8. Evidence Proportionality

Evidence requirements should be proportional to the consequence of the claim.

Small contributions should remain easy to verify.

Large rewards should require stronger evidence.

Possible evidence includes:

- before/after photographs;
- geotagged images;
- time records;
- witness confirmation;
- sensor data;
- project reports;
- completion certificates;
- random inspection.

The objective is to prevent both fraud and unnecessary bureaucracy.

---

## 9. Contribution Difficulty

Community jobs may be classified by difficulty.

A provisional model:

**Level 1 — Simple**
Example: litter collection.

**Level 2 — Basic skill**
Example: painting public benches.

**Level 3 — Moderate expertise**
Example: community gardening.

**Level 4 — Professional**
Example: electrical inspection.

**Level 5 — Highly specialised**
Example: emergency engineering assessment.

Higher difficulty may justify greater recognition.

The precise valuation mechanism remains open.

---

## 10. Matching Participants to Work

The system may match participants to opportunities using:

- location;
- physical capability;
- skills;
- qualifications;
- reputation;
- previous experience;
- availability;
- interests.

The objective is successful completion rather than indiscriminate task assignment.

---

## 11. Accessibility

Ratchet must not assume universal physical capability.

Equivalent contribution pathways should exist for:

- disabled participants;
- elderly participants;
- caregivers;
- remote workers;
- students;
- people with chronic conditions;
- and others whose circumstances limit particular forms of work.

Examples include:

- online mentoring;
- software development;
- translation;
- tutoring;
- research;
- moderation;
- accessibility testing.

The objective remains:

> **Universal opportunity, not uniform output.**

---

## 12. Preventing Manufactured Work

Community contribution creates a serious incentive problem.

A participant must never benefit from manufacturing a problem and then being rewarded for solving it.

Examples include:

- creating graffiti to remove it;
- littering to collect litter;
- damaging infrastructure to repair it;
- manufacturing fake demand;
- repetitive low-value activity.

Potential safeguards include:

- historical analysis;
- anomaly detection;
- location analysis;
- reputation effects;
- investigation;
- penalties for proven abuse.

The protocol rewards solving genuine problems.

---

## 13. Relationship to the Marketplace

The Community Economy should remain complementary to the Marketplace.

A healthy long-term economy should generally have:

**large ordinary marketplace activity**

plus

**targeted community contribution**

rather than allowing protocol-backed work to replace ordinary commerce.

The Marketplace generates private economic activity.

The Community Economy addresses gaps where public value may otherwise remain unrewarded.
